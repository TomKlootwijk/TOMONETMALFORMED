## Page 2: From an idea list to an application book

This companion documents and expands the preceding 18-application reply. The original wording is preserved in a printed appendix and an embedded Markdown witness. Each expanded application now has a use scenario, operating mechanism, model, source mapping, demonstrator, comparison criteria and future extension.

A01-A18 are application identifiers. V01-V07 refer to the existing families in <b>Perpetuum Mobile PM 1.0</b>. The new numbering does not imply eighteen new physical laws or replace the earlier specification.

### The central proposition

A common formal description could connect digital rules, physical memory, geometric transformations, measured feedback and repair. Its value must appear in the useful result: a better experiment, a more reliable action, lower resource use or a clearer explanation of failure.

### How to read the expanded designs

<b>Proposal:</b> a new integration described here. <b>Component evidence:</b> a cited result supporting one ingredient. <b>Model:</b> a conditional equation requiring parameters and an operating region. <b>Demonstrator:</b> a finite experiment that has not been performed for this book. <b>Future:</b> an extension dependent on unresolved engineering.

Equations are compact design contracts. They do not replace a full material model, hardware specification, control proof or qualification process. Numeric acceptance targets are proposed experiment criteria unless a cited source explicitly reports a measured result.

The underlying PM 1.0 PDF is embedded unchanged. It contains the four original source PDFs, the exact SEED contract and scoped reference checks. The new book introduces no claim that these eighteen complete systems have been built.

```text
descriptor + state + input -> proposal -> guard -> commit
actual command -> physical evolution -> measured readback
```

The physical plant keeps evolving while software evaluates a proposal. Digital commit cannot reverse deposited material, heat, charge or motion. A command and its acknowledgment must therefore be separate records, followed by reconciliation with the actual state.

The full causal loop includes sensor, computation, communication and actuator delay. A future machine needs a stability or bounded-error argument for the resulting system. Reusing the same record format does not prove that two physical components are compatible.

## Page 6: Three flagship directions

### 1 / A surface with physical memory

Combine A02 material memory with A03 adaptive tooling. Begin by showing that a measured history-dependent signal improves a bounded contact task. Add deformation only after separating the influence of temperature, shape, load and readout drift. This is a compact place to test whether the source-specific composition adds value.

### 2 / A workshop that changes its capabilities

Combine the adaptive table, A08 experiment automation and A09 inspected fabrication. A fixture is designed, made, measured and used; its later removal or failure remains in the physical record. The result would be a workshop that configures itself for particular tasks within known machine and material limits.

### 3 / A habitat that commissions repairs

Combine A01, A12 and A14: resource monitoring, intermittent-link robots and local maintenance. Begin with an uncrewed installation and a small approved repair catalog. The final ambition is a system that closes inspection, diagnosis, planning, repair and verification while accounting for consumables.

These priorities are editorial judgments about useful research structure, not measured rankings of readiness or commercial value. A01 is the broadest integration; A02 and A03 offer smaller experiments that can expose its assumptions.

A future photonic or acoustic layer can be added through A04/A18 after its bandwidth, calibration, power and conversion costs are known. Its contribution should be evaluated against the same task performed electronically.


## Page 7: A01 / Habitat inspection and repair

<b>Habitats that inspect and repair themselves</b><br/>digital + analog + physical / V02, V05, V06, V07

### A scene to make the idea concrete

An unoccupied remote habitat notices that a service panel is warming differently after each equipment cycle. The proposed system sends an inspection robot, compares measurements with the component history, and selects replacement or local repair. After the work, independent observations determine whether service can resume. Every action consumes tracked energy, material, time and remaining component life.

Detect change → Inspect and plan → Perform repair → Verify and record

### Operating mechanism

The new architecture links telemetry, material-memory features and a geometric inspection map to a bounded repair planner. NASA ISAAC demonstrated robotic inspection triggered by synthetic fault telemetry; its fuller manipulator response was not performed in that ISS scenario [A01R1]. Vision-controlled jetting establishes a separate precedent for measuring deposited geometry and correcting subsequent material delivery [A01R2]. PM would connect these capabilities through typed component identities, joint-test requirements and resource budgets. Issued commands enter the record as pending physical events. Acknowledgments and inspection establish what occurred; their absence cannot erase physical effects or justify assuming success.

```text
E_next = E + E_in - E_service - E_repair - E_waste
repair_ok = geometry_ok AND joint_ok AND budget_ok
history_next = append(history, observed_work)
```

E denotes electrical storage; energy terms are joules. Outgoing terms partition expenditures without counting losses twice. Acceptance flags summarize specified inspections and operating limits, with missing evidence treated as unknown. This is a supervisory contract, not a strength law. A rejected repair retains its material, heat and mechanical consequences.

### Where this framework enters

PM pages 18-19 supply the proposed temporal material readout; pages 24-25 distinguish planned deposition from material already present. Pages 26-29 connect resource accounting with distributed observations. The UGTS registry supplies component and joint definitions, atomOS supplies admission records, and WANTWOMBAN supplies the powered measurement loop. Page 8 requires recovery from measured physical state after an interrupted command.


## Page 8: A01 / Experiment and future

<b>Habitats that inspect and repair themselves</b>

### Minimum demonstrator

Use a non-crewed enclosure containing a replaceable fan duct, a monitored thermal load and a sacrificial repair coupon. Inject repeatable obstructions and sensor faults, then compare fixed maintenance scheduling with inspection-triggered action. Require an independent camera or gauge to verify the result. Add material only to the coupon until separate bond tests justify expansion. Interrupt one acknowledgment deliberately and evaluate whether the system discovers what physically happened before continuing.

| Measure | Proposed comparison or criterion |
| --- | --- |
| Fault localization | On held-out faults, report detection delay and location error against independent inspection, including missed faults and false dispatches at matched sampling effort. |
| Repair validity | Accept recovery only when predefined geometric and joint tests pass; report every failed repair, rework action and unresolved outcome across repeated trials. |
| Resource value | Compare downtime, consumed material and total energy with conventional maintenance under identical faults, counting inspection travel, computation, cleanup and unsuccessful repairs. |

### The failure that matters

A warm patch can indicate changed airflow, sensor drift or an unrelated load. A visually complete deposit can still have a weak bond. The planner therefore separates diagnosis, action and verification. If their assumptions fail, it preserves the observed damage and repair history and suspends the affected service.

### How the future extension would differ

A larger installation could share repair knowledge across habitats while retaining each site&#x27;s actual calibration and wear. Modular service corridors could make inspection and replacement easier than repairing inaccessible structures. The transformative possibility is coordinated upkeep without continuous supervision; the decisive unresolved work is reliable physical diagnosis, joint qualification and resource-aware recovery over long missions.

### The exact role of SEED

SEED reproduces synthetic fault schedules, candidate repair geometries and planning comparisons from a pinned manifest. It does not recreate the habitat&#x27;s damage, wear or sensor readings; those remain measured inputs with timestamps and uncertainty.

## Page 9: A02 / Material-computing robot skin

<b>Robot skin that computes through its material</b><br/>analog + physical + digital / V02, V03, V07

### A scene to make the idea concrete

A gripper touches an unfamiliar object. Instead of reducing each contact to an instantaneous pressure value, the proposed skin retains a fading trace of pressure and temperature changes. That physical history helps a trained readout distinguish a steady hold from repeated rubbing or emerging slip, while a separate controller decides whether and how to adjust the grip.

Sense contact → Evolve material → Read memory → Adjust and verify

### Operating mechanism

Pressure sensors would drive a nearby material-memory layer through calibrated electrical or optical signals. Tan and colleagues demonstrated pressure encoding and integration using an optoelectronic spiking architecture and photomemristors [A02R1]. Du and colleagues demonstrated dynamic-memristor temporal processing with a software readout [A02R2]. The proposed skin combines these component ideas with replaceable sensing patches and explicit preparation procedures. Each patch emits normalized features, uncertainty and age; a digital readout estimates contact class. Local actuation consumes that estimate only through a separately validated policy, rather than treating a classifier score as mechanical authority.

```text
tau_i * zdot_i = -z_i + F_i(p,T,h)
I_i = Read(z_i,p,T; calibration)
y_hat = W_out * [1; Normalize(I)]
```

Here z is a dimensionless fitted memory state, p pressure in pascals, T temperature in kelvin, and I measured current. Positive tau sets a fitted response time. This relaxation model is a local surrogate; material history h and the functions F and Read require device-specific characterization.

### Where this framework enters

PM pages 18-19 define material memory, readout training and comparisons against memoryless sensing. Pages 20-21 separate surface kinematics from force and actuator laws. UGTS contributes patch identities, compatibility and ordered geometry; atomOS records admitted commands and old/new logical state. WANTWOMBAN contributes calibrated readback and retained material history. Pages 28-29 address stale information when patches share observations.

| Measure | Proposed comparison or criterion |
| --- | --- |

### The failure that matters

Material memory can retain irrelevant contacts, drift with temperature or change after deformation. Read pulses may disturb the state being measured. A failed patch must become unavailable rather than silently clear. Grip adjustments remain bounded by independent force and position observations, and a digital reset does not reset the material automatically.

### How the future extension would differ

A distributed computing skin could mix fast electrical memory with slower thermal channels, allowing different patches to specialize in contact history and environmental change. Patches might exchange compact features while keeping raw evidence for uncertain events. The remaining challenge is durable, calibratable computation under real deformation, with a useful overall benefit after electronics and maintenance are included.

### The exact role of SEED

SEED fixes contact sequences, learning splits and digital probe masks for comparisons. Physical initialization still requires a measured preparation procedure; equal seeds cannot guarantee equal conductance, temperature, deformation or contact history across patches.

## Page 11: A03 / A table that becomes tooling

<b>A workshop table that becomes its own tooling</b><br/>digital + physical + analog / V01, V03, V05

### A scene to make the idea concrete

An operator places an irregular component on a flat workbench. The proposed table scans it, raises selected supports and forms a cradle for inspection or light assembly. Where the actuated surface cannot supply the needed feature, a dispenser adds a removable fixture. The table measures contact and loading before presenting the arrangement as usable tooling.

Scan component → Form support → Test contact → Work and inspect

### Operating mechanism

A height-actuated array with compliant contact pads provides a changing support surface. MIT&#x27;s inFORM demonstrated shape-based affordances, constraints and object actuation [A03R1]; it does not establish machining-grade fixtures. PM would add calibrated load sensing, a constrained support planner and optional fixture deposition. The planner first solves for attainable heights, then tests contact under the intended load. Orthogonal Hadamard probe patterns can identify small local response changes, but cannot establish nonlinear strength. A deposited fixture becomes part of the obstruction and cleanup model even when its support test fails.

```text
z_star = argmin_z ||A*z - h_target||^2
subject to z_min<=z<=z_max
accept_support = geometry_ok AND all(f_i<=f_limit_i)
E_in = integral(sum_i V_i*I_i) dt
```

Pin heights z and target heights h are metres; A is a calibrated dimensionless interpolation. After placement, measured forces f in newtons must pass limits for the intended load. Voltage and current determine electrical energy. Feasible geometry alone does not certify stiffness, friction or adhesion.

### Where this framework enters

PM pages 16-17 provide reproducible target geometry and persistent edit identity. Pages 20-21 connect ordered UGTS hinges with calibrated actuation, loading and probe patterns. Pages 24-25 describe guarded deposition and retained physical history. atomOS supplies proposal and disposition records, while WANTWOMBAN supplies the measured feedback pattern. Page 13 requires physical conformance beyond a successful geometric calculation.

## Page 13: A04 / Programmable optical signal network

<b>A programmable optical nervous system</b><br/>analog + digital + physical / V04, V02, V07

### A scene to make the idea concrete

A sensing instrument needs different signal combinations as its task changes. The proposed optical network physically routes coherent inputs, mixes their amplitudes and sends selected outputs to detectors. When a path drifts, the controller measures the change and searches for another configuration. Its changing connections resemble a nervous system functionally, without implying biological cognition or autonomous understanding.

Specify transform → Tune bounded mesh → Measure transfer → Accept or reroute

### Operating mechanism

Tunable interferometers form the physical mixing network, while electronic drivers set phase and coupling. Programmable silicon meshes have demonstrated multiple optical functions [A04R1], and later work demonstrated feedback-based characterization, routing and configuration [A04R2]. PM would represent every usable channel, calibration and failed element in one typed graph. A bounded compiler fits a requested forward transfer matrix to measured device behavior. Coherent detectors verify amplitude and phase; power-only readout cannot certify signed arithmetic. Commands remain proposals until the settled device has been measured, and reconfiguration consumes recorded electrical energy.

```text
b(omega) = S_fwd(omega;theta,T)*a(omega)
S_target = diag(phi,1/phi,1,-1)*H4/phi
S_fwd^dagger*S_fwd <= I
```

Amplitudes a and b are power-normalized, in square-root watts, across four inputs and four outputs. Tuning is held fixed during measurement. The golden ratio phi scales the pinion to a contractive target. Reflection, reverse transmission and loss ports remain separate parts of the physical account.

### Where this framework enters

PM pages 22-23 define the wave architecture and the passive realization constraint for WANTWOMBAN&#x27;s numerical pinion. H4 supplies a concrete orthogonal mixing target; atomOS Boolean XOR is a different operation. UGTS contributes compatible ports and ordered maps. Pages 28-29 add distributed observation age and command history. The proposed compiler must preserve these boundaries rather than treating graph edges as fabricated waveguides.

<b>Remote manufacturing from a &quot;construction seed&quot;</b><br/>digital + physical + analog / V01, V05, V07

### A scene to make the idea concrete

A remote workshop receives a compact construction package for a replacement support. It resolves the exact rulebook, checks available material and measures the installation site. The proposed system regenerates a design, adapts only permitted dimensions, manufactures the support and returns evidence of its actual geometry and tested interfaces. The package coordinates construction; it does not supply matter.

Resolve package → Adapt to site → Build bounded step → Inspect and accept

### Operating mechanism

A construction seed selects a design inside a versioned bounded grammar; the manifest binds geometry, materials, joints and acceptance procedures. NASA ARMADAS provides ground evidence of autonomous assembly from discrete structural blocks [A05R1]. Vision-controlled jetting provides a separate measured-deposition approach [A05R2]. PM would make these alternative manufacturing adapters consume the same typed intent while retaining different physical laws. Each accepted operation references measured predecessor geometry and available resources. A failed joint or interrupted command remains in the site history, and local measurements determine whether to continue, rework or request another design.

```text
plan = Generate(seed, manifest, measured_site)
accept_j = geometry_j AND joint_test_j AND resource_j
mass_next = mass_prev + mass_added - mass_removed
```

The manifest fixes the generator and permitted adaptations; measured site data are explicit inputs. Acceptance flags carry their evidence and unknown status. Mass terms are kilograms of the tracked part, with added retained material distinguished from feedstock waste. Identical plans do not establish identical physical parts.

### Where this framework enters

PM pages 11-12 define exact SEED framing, manifests and replay. Pages 16-17 preserve generated object identity and edits. Pages 24-25 require actual deposition and support status to remain distinct; pages 28-29 address remote command acknowledgment. UGTS supplies grammar and relation records, atomOS supplies admission bookkeeping, and WANTWOMBAN contributes the explicit command-to-measurement boundary rather than an automatic fabrication law.

## Page 17: A06 / VR with moving physical geometry

<b>VR environments with physical, moving geometry</b><br/>digital + physical + analog / V01, V03, V07

### A scene to make the idea concrete

A virtual workshop contains a shelf, a railing and a shaped workpiece. The proposed room moves tracked physical proxies into selected contact locations so the user can touch real surfaces. As the virtual layout changes, the system schedules reachable rearrangements and verifies their actual poses. Visual continuity never becomes permission to assume that physical geometry has teleported.

Predict contact → Reserve and move → Verify readiness → Render interaction

### Operating mechanism

Mobile or height-actuated proxies provide a limited physical vocabulary that a virtual scene can reuse. RoomShift demonstrated furniture-moving robots for room-scale haptics [A06R1], while HapticBots explored multiple moving, shape-changing contact proxies [A06R2]. PM would add persistent virtual identities, bounded physical mapping and explicit contact-readiness records. A planner predicts upcoming interactions, reserves movement corridors and issues commands only within the characterized tracking and stopping envelope. The renderer obtains readiness from measured pose and settled motion, not from the command queue. Unreachable contacts receive a different interaction rather than fabricated physical support.

```text
d_clear >= d_stop + e_tracking + v_rel*latency
proxy_target = Map(virtual_contact, calibrated_room)
contact_ready = pose_ok AND settled AND tracked
```

Distances are metres; v_rel bounds relative closing speed throughout latency in seconds. d_stop bounds remaining relative closing distance during stopping, including continued user motion. Tracking error and stopping behavior require measured bounds. This simplified corridor condition needs geometry-specific verification; it cannot alone guarantee collision avoidance.

### Where this framework enters

PM pages 16-17 supply reproducible virtual geometry, stable identities and edits. Pages 20-21 distinguish an ordered hinge model from the mechanics of moving furniture or proxies. Pages 28-29 preserve observation age and interrupted-command history. UGTS supplies compatible contact mappings; atomOS records proposals and readiness. WANTWOMBAN contributes calibrated feedback, while page 8 prevents digital rollback from erasing real motion.
